VSD Student Learning Workspace — Prompts Tab Specs

The detailed spec for the Prompts tab (tab B in ../APP_SPECS.txt). APP_SPECS.txt covers the core app
and the Workspace tab; this file covers how the Prompts tab works. Why students can see and edit the
prompts is in APP_SPECS.txt (PRODUCT).


PURPOSE

The Prompts tab shows and edits the instructions the AI follows when it gives feedback in a lesson.
Anyone, students included, can read and change them: shaping these rules is part of learning how to
prompt AI. Each lesson has its own prompts (its own AI role, questions, and rubric).


LAYOUT

Header:
  - "PROMPT DESIGN." title (the tab stays "Prompts"; the lesson is picked in the top bar, above the
    sections), and a short introduction that frames the AI as a critical-thinking partner, not a
    tutor, and says anyone can change its instructions: "The AI here is told not to give answers.
    Instead, it asks questions, points out gaps, and challenges your reasoning, following the
    instructions below. Anyone, students included, can read and change them: deciding how an AI
    should challenge you is part of learning to work with one."

Body, two columns:
  - Left: three editors in the order they are sent: System, Questions to ask right under it (they
    are sent inside the system prompt), then User. The Versions tab uses the same order.
  - Right: the Preview panel.

Save bar, pinned to the bottom of the tab: the version being edited, what changed, and the controls
that save it (see SAVE BAR).


THE THREE PARTS

- System: who the AI is and its rules. Sent as written. Hint: "The AI's role, tone, and rules."
- Questions to ask: the questions the AI can ask about the student's work, one per line, in two
  lists (see QUESTIONS TO ASK). Hint: "Questions the AI can ask about the work, one per line.
  They're sent with the System prompt: the ones for every step, plus the ones for the step being
  checked. Add, remove, or reword them to change what the AI pushes you on."
- User: a template sent with each feedback request. Its {{variables}} are filled in (see below).

Variables are only filled in the User prompt. A {{name}} in the System prompt or the questions is
sent exactly as typed.


STARTING PROMPTS

Each lesson starts with one prompt set. The AI is framed as a critical thinking partner, not a
tutor:
  - System: "You are a critical thinking partner for students ..." Don't give answers or list what
    is missing (value scenarios: don't write or rewrite scenarios); ask short, specific questions
    that make the student look again at their own work; start with one specific thing done well,
    then two or three questions, no more; the reasoning should stay theirs.
  - Questions for every step (both lessons): who is affected without ever using it (value
    scenarios: and whose story isn't told yet); what those people would say about the student's
    choices (or this future); which of the student's assumptions they would question.
  - Questions for each step: two or three per step, written from that step's task and objectives
    (e.g. Stakeholder Tokens step 3: who is affected without ever using it; who was left out
    because they seemed far away or hard to see; who is excluded by the design, and is that a
    choice). All 17 steps (9 + 8) have their own list. The full text is in each lesson's
    content file, frontend/content/<lesson>/prompts.json (see frontend/content/README.md).
  - Questions are written in the second person ("your map"), as the AI would ask them.


QUESTIONS TO ASK

Two lists, so the questions adapt to the activity being checked:
  - For every step: the general critical-thinking moves, one list per lesson.
  - For this step: questions for one activity only, one list per step of the lesson.

Editing:
  - "For every step" is always shown.
  - "For this step · Step N · Title" shows the list for the step open in the Workspace, the same
    step the preview uses. There is no step picker in the Prompts tab itself: to edit another
    step's questions, pick that step in the step trail under the sections (see APP_SPECS.txt; the
    hint under the box says "To edit another step's questions, pick it in the steps at the top.").
  - Edits to any step's list count as unsaved changes and are saved with the version. Lists for
    several steps can be edited before saving one version.

Sent inside the system prompt, after the System text, each list under the app's heading:

    <System text>

    Questions you can draw on at any step:
    <the questions for every step>

    Questions for this step:
    <the questions for the step being checked>

  An empty list adds nothing. Only the checked step's list is sent, never other steps': a question
  about a later step (e.g. tensions while the student is naming direct stakeholders) would hand
  them that step's work early, the same reason student work is limited to the step.

Why both lists and the task/objectives variables: the variables tell the AI what the step asks and
what good work looks like (its rubric, and how it spots "one thing done well"); the questions tell
it how to push the student's thinking. Task and objectives are lesson content, shared with the step
card; the questions are prompts anyone can edit.
- Why questions and not an example: each question is one thing the AI may do, so students can add,
  remove or reword one and see the effect, which is the prompting lesson. An example would also show
  tone and length, but the AI tends to copy its specifics; the System rules cover tone and length.
- Why not an assistant message: the third box was first an "Assistant" box meant as a prefill (the
  start of the model's reply), but current Claude models reject a request that ends with an
  assistant message, so a filled-in box would have made every feedback request fail.
- One definition of the sent text: systemPromptParts in promptService. The preview uses it, and the
  backend sends the same text.
- Saved versions keep the questions in PromptSet.questions (every step) and
  PromptSet.stepQuestions (by activity id). Versions saved in a browser under the box's earlier
  names (`assistant`, then `example`) load with that text in "For every step", so nothing written
  is lost; they start with no per-step questions.
- The Versions tab lists, in the order they are sent: System, Questions to ask · For every step,
  Questions to ask · Step N · Title for each step that has questions, then User.


TEMPLATE VARIABLES

  {{activity_title}}    The activity's title.
  {{activity_prompt}}   The activity's task description (its prompt).
  {{objectives}}        The activity's learning objectives, one "- objective" per line.
  {{student_work}}      The student's work on the activity in plain language, limited to the
                        activity's step (see STUDENT WORK UP TO THE STEP and STUDENT WORK IN PLAIN
                        LANGUAGE). "(no student work yet)" when there is none.

- Names are matched exactly: {{name}}, letters, digits and underscores, no spaces.
- A name that isn't one of these four is left exactly as typed, so the gap stays visible.
- There is no variable for the raw JSON: the AI gets the plain-language form, and the JSON is what
  gets stored. (A {{student_work_json}} variable for comparing the two could be added later.)
- The mapping is defined once (templateVariables in promptService). The preview uses it, and the
  backend fills the same variables the same way for real feedback.


STUDENT WORK UP TO THE STEP

Lessons whose steps share one board (activities with the same stateKey) also share one saved state,
so the saved state can hold work from steps after the one being looked at. {{student_work}} only
holds what exists by the activity's step: the same per-step view the worked example uses (each
activity model's forStep). For example, in Stakeholder Tokens step 2, indirect stakeholders and the
"overlooked" marks are left out, because they belong to step 3.

- This applies to both the student's work and the worked example.
- It is decided by what each step introduces, not by when the student did the work: an edit made
  while revisiting an earlier step counts for that step.
- Work from earlier steps carries over: on a shared board, anything saved in an earlier step is
  already the student's work for later steps.
- Activities that don't share a board are unchanged.
- Trimming is done in the frontend (workUpToStep, next to the activity registry), so the backend
  never needs to know how an activity works.


STUDENT WORK IN PLAIN LANGUAGE

The work is always stored as JSON (in the browser now, on the backend later). For the prompt,
{{student_work}} writes it out in plain language, so the AI and the preview read the same thing:
what the student made, without ids or board positions. For example, Stakeholder Tokens step 5:

    Starting point (technology): Smart doorbell camera that records the front door ...

    Direct stakeholders:
    - Homeowners — core
    ...
    Indirect stakeholders:
    - People experiencing homelessness — excluded, hidden or overlooked

    Relationships:
    - Homeowners —shares footage with→ Police      (one way)
    - Homeowners ←share a home→ Household members   (mutual)

    Harms and benefits:
    - Homeowners:
      - Benefit: Sees who is at the door from anywhere
      - Harm: Clips stored on company servers could be leaked

- Only the parts the step has reached appear, in the order the steps add them (values on harms and
  benefits from step 6, owners and next steps from step 9; for value scenarios, each card's fields
  as the steps add them, and tensions at step 8). A reached part with nothing in it reads
  "(none yet)"; an empty field reads "(not written yet)".
- Each activity writes out its own work (describeWork in its model.ts, listed in
  activities/workText.ts). Activities without one are sent as JSON.
- The wording (section headings, ring names, "Harm"/"Benefit", "(none yet)", ...) is lesson
  content: content/<lesson>/work-description.json. Labels left out of the file keep the activity's
  default wording; a key the activity doesn't use is reported as a content mistake.
- The preview shows exactly what is sent.
- The backend never converts work: the frontend sends both the JSON (stored) and the text (used for
  {{student_work}}). See BACKEND_SPEC.txt.


VARIABLE CHIPS

- Directly under the User editor: "Click to insert:" and one chip per variable.
- Clicking a chip inserts {{name}} at the User editor's cursor, replacing any selection, and puts the
  cursor right after it. The editor remembers its cursor after losing focus, so the insert lands
  where the student was typing.
- Each variable goes in once: a chip is unavailable while its variable is anywhere in the User
  prompt (tooltip: "Already in the User prompt"), and becomes available again as soon as the
  variable is removed. With the default template all four chips start unavailable.


HIGHLIGHTING IN THE USER EDITOR

- As the student types, each known {{variable}} shows in the variable blue and each unknown name in
  the issue color with a wavy underline, matching the warnings.
- A textarea can't style parts of its text, so a copy of the text is drawn behind it with the
  variables colored, and the textarea's own text is transparent. Typing, the caret, selection,
  undo, scrolling and resizing stay the textarea's. Selected text gets a light blue tint so the
  colors show through.
- The copy must lay text out exactly like the textarea (font, padding, borders, wrapping, scrollbar
  gutter). If the colors ever look shifted or doubled, that alignment is what to check.


TEMPLATE WARNINGS

Shown in the issue color under the variable chips, as the student types:
  - Unknown name: "{{name}} isn't a variable, so the AI gets it exactly as typed. Check the
    spelling." One line per distinct name.
  - Missing work: "There's no {{student_work}}, so the AI won't see the student's work and can't
    give feedback on it."

Shown under the System prompt, "For every step", and "For this step", for any {{name}} typed there:
  - "{{student_work}} only works in the User prompt. Here, the AI gets it exactly as typed." (Several
    names are listed together.) Variables are only filled in the User prompt.

They inform; they never block saving, since students are meant to experiment. They are announced
to screen readers when they appear.


PREVIEW

- Shows the two prompts as they would be sent (an empty one is skipped): System, with the questions
  for every step and for the open step inside it, and User, with the template filled in. The app's
  headings before the questions are muted and italic (tooltip: "Added by the app before your
  questions").
- Follows the step open in the Workspace: all four variables come from that activity. The header
  reads "What the AI receives", with "The full prompt the AI gets when work on this step is
  checked, as two messages. Only the text in the boxes is sent." in the header under it (the System
  and User labels name the two messages); the step itself
  is in the step trail. Each prompt sits in a box, greyer than the editors' paper so it reads as
  read only; labels and hints outside the boxes are the app's notes and are not sent. Until the Workspace has
  opened a step, the lesson's first activity is used.
- The step open in each lesson is one shared choice in the app shell, the same way the lesson is, so
  the Workspace and the Prompts tab can't disagree.
- Text filled in from a variable is blue; hovering it shows which variable it came from. Unknown names
  show in the issue color with a wavy underline (tooltip: "Not a variable: sent as typed"). A hint under
  the User label (only the User prompt has variables): "Blue text is filled in from a variable.
  Hover over it to see which one."
- The student's work is read again each time the Prompts tab is opened, so changes made in the
  Workspace always show up.

"Copy prompt" (in the preview header, with a copy icon): copies both messages as plain text, each
under its label ("System:", then a blank line, "User:"), exactly as the preview shows them, with the
sample currently chosen. So students can paste the prompt into another chat tool. The label reads
"Copied" for two seconds ("Couldn't copy" if the browser refuses), and the result is announced to
screen readers. Disabled when both prompts are empty.

Sample switch ("My work | Worked example", beside the User label: it only changes {{student_work}}):
  - Picks what fills {{student_work}} in the preview. The option in use is filled dark.
  - Starts on My work. The choice is kept across steps and lessons while the app is open.
  - An option the step lacks is unavailable, and the preview uses the other one:
      My work, no saved work: "Start this lesson in the Workspace to see your work here. Work from
        earlier steps carries over." (on a shared board), or "Start this step in the Workspace to
        see your work here." (otherwise).
      Worked example, none for the step: "This step has no worked example."
  - With neither, both are unavailable and {{student_work}} reads "(no student work yet)".
  - The switch only affects the preview. "Check my work" in the Workspace always sends the student's
    own work.
  - Saved work belongs to the browser and the exact web address it was made at: work done at another
    address (127.0.0.1 instead of localhost, another port, the published site) doesn't show up.


TOOLTIPS

- The tab uses its own quick tooltip (a data-tip attribute) instead of the browser's title tooltip,
  which waits about a second and can't be made faster. It appears about 0.15 s after hover or
  keyboard focus.
- Styled like the board's token tooltip: solid white, ink border and text, square corners. It opens
  above its element; in the preview header it opens leftward so it stays on the page.
- Unavailable controls that need a tooltip (the sample switch, used chips) are marked aria-disabled
  instead of disabled, so they still show their tooltip on hover and focus; clicking them does
  nothing. They are dimmed by text color, not opacity, which would fade the tooltip too.


SAVE BAR

Pinned to the bottom of the tab (sticky in the tab's scroll area) and spanning the page, so what
changed and the controls that save it stay in view while editing. Why not in the header: the header
scrolls away as soon as an editor is clicked, taking the changes and the save button with it.

  - Left, the status: "Editing from version N", with an "Unsaved changes" badge while the draft
    differs from version N. Under it, "Changed: ..." listing each changed part (see WHAT CHANGED),
    or "No changes yet."
  - Right, the controls: a version name field, "Discard changes", and "Save as new version" (both
    buttons disabled when nothing has changed).
  - Name field placeholder: Name this version, e.g. “Sharper questions” (labelled "Version name"
    for screen readers). Why not "What changed?": the bar already lists what changed, and the name
    is what the Versions tab shows under the version number. The example steers people toward
    names that say something, not "v2 final". It is short enough to fit a phone-width field.
  - While there are unsaved changes, the bar's top edge is a thicker plum line.
  - After saving: "Saved as version N." (bold) and "You're now editing from it." for four seconds,
    or until the next edit, then back to "Editing from version N" / "No changes yet." The save is
    announced to screen readers.
  - Narrow screens (800px or less): with nothing to save, the bar shows only the "Editing from
    version N" line, so it takes less of a small screen. The field and buttons come back with the
    first edit.


WHAT CHANGED

The draft is compared with the version its session started from, part by part.

  - Each editor whose text differs shows a "Changed" badge beside its label: System, For every
    step, the open step's list, User.
  - The save bar lists every changed part, in the order they are sent: System, Questions for every
    step, Step N questions (each step whose list differs, in lesson order), User. It includes steps
    that aren't open, which is how edits to other steps' questions are seen before saving or
    discarding.
  - Each item in the list is a link to its editor: it focuses the editor and scrolls it to the
    middle of the window, clear of the save bar. "Step N questions" opens that step first,
    everywhere, as picking it in the step trail does.
  - Not highlighted inside the editors or the preview. A textarea can't color parts of its own text
    without drawing a copy behind it (see HIGHLIGHTING IN THE USER EDITOR), and colors would
    distract while writing. In the preview, highlighted text already means "filled in from a
    variable". A line-by-line comparison belongs to the Versions tab (Compare; see LATER).
  - The list is worked out in one place, promptChanges in promptService, which returns each change
    with its part and step, so the Versions tab can use it later. For now it is shown only while
    editing: saved versions don't record it yet (see LATER).


EDITING, SAVING, AND VERSIONS

- Each lesson has its own editing session (the version it started from, the draft, the version
  name), kept while the app is open, so switching lessons from any tab never discards unsaved edits.
  A lesson with unsaved edits is marked with a dot in the lesson switcher (see APP_SPECS.txt).
- "Save as new version" saves the draft with the version name (or "Version N" when the name is
  empty). It never overwrites an existing version. Versions are numbered within each lesson.
- "Discard changes" asks for confirmation, then returns the draft and version name to the version
  the session started from.
- Closing or reloading the page with unsaved edits in any lesson makes the browser ask first.
- Storage (placeholder until the backend): the starting versions live in code (mock data); versions
  saved in the browser are kept in localStorage ("prompt-versions") and listed after them, so they
  survive a reload but are only visible in that browser. Unsaved drafts don't survive a reload.
- Caution: saved versions are numbered after the starting versions, so adding a starting version to
  a lesson later gives it the same number and id as that browser's first saved version there. Edit
  an existing starting version instead, or clear "prompt-versions" after adding one. This goes away
  once the backend numbers versions.


CONNECTION TO FEEDBACK

- "Check my work" in the Workspace sends the student's work limited to the activity's step, exactly
  what the preview shows for My work.
- The feedback request uses the lesson's latest saved prompt version and records its id on the
  result, so feedback can be traced to the prompts that produced it.
- Placeholder: no model is connected yet, so the prompts don't change the feedback students see.
  Later, the backend fills the User template with the variables above and sends the three prompts
  to the model.


VISUAL STYLE

Follows VISUAL STYLE in APP_SPECS.txt (mauve ground, Cormorant Garamond, ink text), plus:
  - Variable blue #1F4F99 (--variable, about 7.5:1 on the editor background) wherever a variable
    appears: the chips' text, {{names}} in the User editor, and filled-in text in the preview.
  - Issue color #9C3328 (--issue) for unknown names and the template warnings.
  - The lesson's accent color marks the editors' left edge and the preview's top band.
  - Monospace for the editors, chips, and preview text.
  - The save bar is solid #B0A9B2 (the bottom of the mauve ground) with a thin top border and no
    shadow, so the editors scrolling behind it don't show through.


ACCESSIBILITY

- Every control works from the keyboard; tooltips also show on keyboard focus.
- Template warnings are a live region (announced when they appear).
- Unavailable controls use aria-disabled, so they stay focusable and say why they are unavailable.
- The sample switch is a labelled group of toggle buttons (aria-pressed).
- The colored copy behind the User editor is hidden from screen readers; the textarea is the editor.
- "Saved as version N" is announced through a status region, since the visible line changing isn't
  read out on its own.
- The links in the save bar's change list are ink with a plum underline: plum text is about 4.2:1
  on the bar, under the 4.5:1 needed at that size. Ink is about 9:1. The save message is bold ink
  for the same reason (the green --praise is about 3.4:1).
